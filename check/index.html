      What the check measures, and how

      The test sound lasts 4.6 seconds: a short chirp, a sweep from 100 Hz to 21 kHz, and the chirp again. The microphone listens a second before it starts.

      • Noise: the second of quiet before the sound.
      • Band and SNR: dividing what came back by the sweep gives the response of the speaker, the room and the microphone at every frequency. The SNR is for a signal as loud as the sweep (its RMS level, 9 dB below full scale), spread evenly over 2.3 to 20.5 kHz, as the modems' wide band is.
      • Shannon limit: the most bits per second any modem sending that flat spectrum could carry over this link: the sum of log2(1 + SNR) across the band. Real modems reach a fraction of it.
      • Echo: the same response, read in time: how long the room keeps ringing, until 30 dB below the direct sound.
      • Distortion: a loud speaker adds harmonics. A sweep puts them at their own place in time, so they can be measured apart.
      • Clock: the two chirps are 3.8 s apart on the player's clock; the listener counts its own samples between them.
      • Delay (this device alone): from the moment the page asks the speaker to play to the moment the microphone hears it.
      • Checks: each chirp must stand out from the sound just before it, so a steady tone or music is not taken for the test sound. Another test sound in the same recording, a level change between the two chirps, and 44.1 kHz taken for 48 kHz are reported.

      The same analysis runs in Python on any recording: python devcheck.py analyse rec.wav.
